Scope

Package scope

One message schedule ties together the lobby directory, floor markers, corridor directions, and room names so the building reads as a single system.

Lobby directory signs

Building and tenant directories at the lobby, sized to the wall and to how the list will change after move-in.

Floor, elevator, and stair IDs

Elevator jamb designations with tactile floor identifiers on both jambs, including a star at the main entry. Stair doors get the floor-identification sign the Building Code sizes and places, plus a separate tactile floor sign. These are not the lobby directory.

Corridor decision points

Interior directional signage and corridor signs where paths split, at elevator lobbies, and along the accessible route.

Room ID signs

Room names and numbers for offices, suites, and support spaces. Permanent room identification is planned apart from directional copy.

Restrooms and exits

Restroom IDs and tactile exit-door signs the drawings call out. Illuminated exit signs stay with the electrical scope.

Phased openings

Directory inserts, temporary directions, and later message updates when floors or tenants open in phases.

Navigation is not the same as room identification. Directional and informational signs generally use visual characters. Signs that identify a permanent room or space can require tactile characters and Grade 2 Braille. A directory is not automatically in that second group. Which signs need Braille is decided sign by sign, with the architect or code professional.

How the system is planned

Arc starts from decision points: the lobby, each elevator bank, corridor intersections, and the room itself. The sign schedule lists every message, location, and sign type so a multi-floor fit-out stays one package instead of a stack of one-off orders.

Finishes, insert systems, and mounting are shown on the shop drawings for architect and GC review before fabrication through licensed partners.

Room identification

ADA and tactile room IDs

Directional signs and tactile room identification signs follow different rules. Arc keeps them distinct on the schedule and does not treat a whole wayfinding set as tactile.

Permanent room signs may need raised characters and Grade 2 Braille; corridor and directory signs usually stay visual only. Restroom signs that identify a permanent room are tactile. Stair doors get the floor-identification sign the Building Code sizes and places, plus a separate tactile floor sign. Illuminated exit signs follow the egress rules for letter size and emergency power. They are not the tactile room sign.

Code references
  • 2010 ADA Standards §216.2 (permanent room and space identification) and §216.3 (directional and informational signs)
  • Raised characters and Braille: §703.2 and §703.3; latch-side mounting and clear floor space: §703.4
  • BC 1111 covers the dynamic accessibility symbol and the directional signs to accessible elements. Tactile room signs are a separate decision under the ADA Standards and ICC A117.1. Elevator jamb designations are tactile characters on both jambs, including a tactile star at the main entry, and they are not the lobby directory.
Process

Plans and a survey come first. Then circulation and naming, a message schedule and shop drawings, architect and GC review, fabrication through licensed partners, and installation with punch-list coordination.

  1. 01

    Survey

    Plans plus a look at doors, latch-side wall space, sightlines, and elevator entrances.

  2. 02

    Naming

    Circulation and room names checked against how people actually walk the floor.

  3. 03

    Drawings

    Message schedule and shop drawings for architect and GC review.

  4. 04

    Fabricate

    Directories, floor IDs, corridor signs, and room signs through licensed partners, including tactile signs the drawings call for.

  5. 05

    Install

    Installation through licensed partners and punch list, including updates when a floor opens later.

Fabrication and installation are through our licensed partners. Arc does not file permits or stamp drawings. Exterior sign filings are covered on sign permits and shop drawings.

FAQ

Wayfinding questions

Do all interior wayfinding signs need Braille?

No. The 2010 ADA Standards cover permanent room and space identification in §216.2, and directional and informational signs in §216.3 through visual characters. A lobby directory or a corridor direction is not automatically a tactile sign.

Which signs usually need tactile text and Grade 2 Braille?

Permanent room and space identification can require raised characters duplicated in contracted Grade 2 Braille. Directional copy is not automatically tactile or set in Braille. Building directories, occupant names, and company names and logos are treated differently from permanent room signs.

Where should a tactile room sign be mounted?

Section 703.4 addresses latch-side location, mounting height, and clear floor space beyond the door swing. The project architect or code professional should confirm the condition at each door, including doors without latch-side wall space.

Does NYC have additional accessibility signage rules?

BC 1111 covers the dynamic accessibility symbol and the directional signs to accessible elements. Tactile room signs are a separate decision under the ADA Standards and ICC A117.1. Elevator jamb designations are tactile characters on both jambs, including a tactile star at the main entry, and they are not the lobby directory.

Can Arc coordinate a multi-floor package?

Yes. Arc reviews plans, surveys conditions, prepares a message schedule and shop drawings, and coordinates fabrication and installation through licensed partners across floors, including phased openings.

What should I send for a quote?
