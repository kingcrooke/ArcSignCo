Where it goes

Restaurant, retail, entrance, or an event

The door, the sidewalk, and the property line decide which drawing is even allowed. A room inside the building line is not the same filing as a box on the sidewalk.

Restaurant

An airlock at the door, so the wind does not blow straight into the dining room. Sidewalk seating is a separate question, and a new sidewalk cafe cannot be fully enclosed.

Retail

A small entry at one door, with the business name on the header. Send a front photo and a side photo, and the width of the opening.

Building entrance

A lobby-size vestibule, or a glass-and-aluminum entry if the enclosure stays up. A unit that stays up is an alteration, not a seasonal storm enclosure.

Events

A tent with sidewalls on private property. That is a Department of Buildings question, not a Dining Out license.

Permits

Sidewalk, roadway, and private property are different filings

A seasonal box at the door is not a dining room on the sidewalk. Rules change. Confirm the current text with the agency and the licensed applicant. Sources checked October 21, 2026.

Arc does not file permits; licensed partners file.

NYC storm vestibules, Dining Out, heat, and landmarks

Building Code §3202.3.2 allows a temporary storm enclosure from November 15 through the following April 15. It has to come down, and it has to meet the code, including the fire-resistance rating of the building and Chapter 11. For an eating or drinking establishment, §3202.3.2.1 allows up to 25 square feet of projection when 5 feet of clear sidewalk remains and the door clearances in Chapter 11 and ICC A117.1 §404 are met. For other establishments, §3202.3.2.2 limits the projection to 18 inches beyond the street line. NYC311 still describes the 18-inch limit and does not mention the 25-square-foot allowance. The code text controls. NYC311 also takes complaints that a storm enclosure has no permit. Administrative Code §28-105.4 does not list storm enclosures as permit-exempt.

A new sidewalk cafe cannot be fully enclosed. The perimeter barrier is limited to 2 feet 6 inches, excluding plantings, and has to be removable. A roadway cafe is open air. NYC311 says no fully enclosed structures. A roadway cafe may use a cover and a street-side screen up to 6 feet that is not opaque. Sidewalk cafes can run year-round. Roadway cafes do not operate from November 30 through March 31. Year-round roadway license applications open in early September 2026. Existing enclosed sidewalk cafes built under a Department of Buildings permit before March 16, 2020 are a separate case. A sealed room inside the property line is a Department of Buildings filing, not a Dining Out license.

Heaters need Department of Buildings and Fire Department approval. Buildings Bulletin 2024-005 does not allow open-flame natural gas heaters or propane heaters in sidewalk, roadway, and outdoor cafes. A natural-gas radiant heater may be allowed for a sidewalk cafe or an outdoor cafe inside the property line, not for a roadway cafe. Electric heat is a licensed master electrician and an electrical permit. If the enclosure includes an awning or canopy, Building Code §3105.3 applies to that awning or canopy, including the fabric test in that section. Chapter 32 does not name NFPA 701. Light-transmitting plastics have a specification in §2606.4. The 50-foot and 300-square-foot limits are in §2605.2, for exterior plastic veneer. §2607.1 does not allow light-transmitting plastic wall panels in Group A-2, and many restaurants are A-2. Whether a seasonal sidewalk vestibule is that kind of wall panel is confirmed with the licensed applicant.

On a landmark or in a historic district, a vestibule, awning, cover, or conduit attached to the building needs a Landmarks Preservation Commission permit, filed through Portico. The storefronts page says any change to the exterior of a storefront requires a permit, and it still says an unenclosed sidewalk cafe needs LPC and the Department of Transportation. The 2024 amendment to 63 RCNY §2-23 says a sidewalk or roadway cafe that meets the Department of Transportation rules, and is not physically attached to the building or the historic paving, does not need an LPC application. Enclosed sidewalk cafes are not covered by that exception. Check the address. Filing questions belong on sign permits and shop drawings.

FAQ

Enclosure questions

What is the difference between a vestibule and a dining enclosure?

A vestibule is the small room at the door, an air buffer so the wind does not blow straight into the dining room or the shop. A dining enclosure is a cover or a set of panels around seating. On a New York sidewalk, the vestibule is the temporary storm enclosure in Building Code §3202.3.2. A dining setup on the sidewalk or the roadway is the Dining Out program, and a new one cannot be fully enclosed.

When can a temporary storm vestibule stand on an NYC sidewalk?

Building Code §3202.3.2 allows a temporary storm enclosure from November 15 through the following April 15. It has to come down after that, and it has to meet the code, including the fire-resistance rating of the building and Chapter 11. For an eating or drinking establishment, §3202.3.2.1 allows up to 25 square feet of projection when 5 feet of clear sidewalk remains and the door maneuvering clearances in Chapter 11 and ICC A117.1 §404 are met. For other establishments, §3202.3.2.2 limits the projection to 18 inches beyond the street line. NYC311 still describes the 18-inch limit and does not mention the 25-square-foot restaurant allowance. The code text controls, and the licensed applicant confirms the measurement for the address. NYC311 also takes complaints that a storm enclosure does not have a permit. Administrative Code §28-105.4 does not list storm enclosures as permit-exempt, so this page does not claim a seasonal vestibule is permit-free.

Can a new sidewalk or roadway cafe be fully enclosed?

No. The Department of Transportation does not allow new enclosed sidewalk cafes. A sidewalk cafe may not be fully enclosed, and the perimeter barrier is limited to 2 feet 6 inches, excluding plantings, and has to be removable. A roadway cafe is open air. NYC311 says roadway setups cannot be fully enclosed structures. A roadway cafe may use a cover and a street-side clear screen up to 6 feet that is not opaque. Existing enclosed sidewalk cafes that were built under a Department of Buildings permit before March 16, 2020, and lawfully operated between March 16, 2016 and March 16, 2020, are a separate case. A sealed room inside the property line is a Department of Buildings filing, not a Dining Out license.

What if the building is a landmark or in a historic district?

A vestibule, awning, cover, or conduit attached to the building needs a Landmarks Preservation Commission permit, filed through Portico. The storefronts page says any change to the exterior of a storefront requires a permit, and it still says an unenclosed sidewalk cafe needs a permit from LPC and from the Department of Transportation. The May 2024 amendment to 63 RCNY §2-23 says a sidewalk or roadway cafe that meets the Department of Transportation rules, and is not physically attached to the building or the historic paving, does not need an LPC application. Enclosed sidewalk cafes are not covered by that exception. Check the address on the LPC map. Arc does not file permits; licensed partners file.

Can the enclosure be heated?

Heaters need Department of Buildings and Fire Department approval. Buildings Bulletin 2024-005 does not allow open-flame natural gas heaters or propane heaters in sidewalk, roadway, and outdoor cafes. A natural-gas radiant heater may be allowed for a sidewalk cafe or an outdoor cafe inside the property line, not for a roadway cafe, and it needs a licensed master plumber, a Department of Buildings permit, and Fire Department permits and a Certificate of Fitness before it operates. Electric heat is a licensed master electrician and an electrical permit. Do not plan on propane. Older Open Restaurants guidance is not the current rule.

Seasonal or permanent?

A seasonal sidewalk storm vestibule stands from November 15 through April 15 and then comes down. A permanent enclosure stays up and is an alteration. In DOB NOW, Alteration-CO (formerly Alt-1) is the job type when the work changes occupancy or use, makes a major change to exits, is inconsistent with the current certificate of occupancy, changes the number of stories, or has to meet New Building requirements. Other alterations are filed as Alteration, which covers the former Alt-2 and Alt-3. A permanent vestibule is not automatically a minor alteration. The licensed applicant confirms the job type.
