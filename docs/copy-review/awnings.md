## New awning, recover, or replacement

A storefront awning recover can keep a sound frame. A replacement storefront awning, or new framing, is the path when the frame or the projection no longer fits the storefront. Custom business awnings start from the opening, not from a catalog nickname.

A recover is worth pricing when the frame is straight, the attachments are sound, and the projection, clearance, and profile can stay. New framing or a full replacement is the better scope when the frame is bent, rusted, or undersized, or when the drop, projection, or shape has to change. Send dimensions, straight-on and side photos, the projection, the clearance to the sidewalk, frame details, and the finishes you want to keep or change.

### Traditional slope

A straight roof, with or without side panels. The usual storefront profile, and the one landmark criteria generally describe for a new fixed awning.

### Quarter-round

A convex front on flat ends, built from curved bows. Graphics usually sit on the valance, where the face is easiest to read from the sidewalk.

### Dome

A quarter-sphere over a door or a short window. An elongated dome stretches that curve for a longer front. Some shops call the long version a bullnose. The shop drawing names the profile.

### Retractable

Fabric on a roller with lateral arms, so the cover can roll back. Useful where a fixed projection is not what the landlord or the landmark criteria allow.

Open sides show the frame and arms. Closed sides fill the ends with cover fabric and block more sun and rain. The shop drawing says which one, because it changes the frame, the fabric count, and how the awning meets the piers.

These side views are planning sketches, not fabrication details. Projection, drop, and valance height are set from the survey.

## Sign text and layout

Awning signs for New York storefronts are usually the business name on the valance, where it can be read from the sidewalk. Contrast, letter size, and sightlines matter as much as the wording.

Copy, a logo, or both can sit on a rigid valance or on a loose skirt. A light name on a dark valance, or the reverse, is the usual way to keep the words readable. The slope of the roof is a weak place for a long line of text, and at a landmark the guidance generally puts lettering on the skirt only.

Non-illuminated copy that the Zoning Resolution allows on an awning is limited to the name or address of the building or of an establishment in it, with letters no taller than 12 inches and no more than 12 square feet of copy. Illuminated copy, or copy past those limits, is reviewed as a sign. Sign text on awnings can therefore need its own filing, separate from the awning itself. Arc does not treat one drawing as an approval.

A tall front face, sometimes called a waterfall or a box, is one way to light graphics from inside. That face is a sign scope, with electrical coordination, not a plain fabric recover. When the facade should carry a separate illuminated identity, the storefront sign is [channel letters](/channel-letters/).

### Straight valance

A flat band. The most common edge, and the simplest place for a single line of lettering.

### Scalloped

Repeated round lobes. The pattern should end evenly at both corners so the name stays centered.

### Wave

A soft rise and fall. A low wave reads almost straight from across the street. A deeper wave is more decorative and leaves less room for letters.

## DOB, DOT, landlord, and LPC coordination

An awning attached only to the building is a different project from a pole-supported sidewalk canopy. Landlord rules and landmark review sit on top of that split. Arc coordinates. The agencies decide.

- **Department of Buildings.** DOB describes an awning or a replacement canopy under General Construction. Lettering is a separate or subsequent Sign work type. Arc prepares drawings and information for the licensed applicant and does not file as that applicant.
- **Building Code clearances.** Provisions for a storefront awning over the sidewalk describe a projection of no more than 8 feet beyond the street line, frame clearance of at least 8 feet, a flexible valance that may come down to 7 feet, and an awning box or cover of no more than 12 inches. An awning over a window or door is described at no more than 5 feet. The awning has to be supported from the building to use those allowances. Confirm the current text for the address.
- **Department of Transportation.** A cover on posts from the entrance to the curb is a sidewalk canopy. NYC311 puts that permit with DOT, not with the building-attached awning path. Width, height, length, and lettering rules are different. Confirm which structure the storefront actually is.
- **Landlord awning criteria.** Leases often limit projection, color, copy, lighting, and how the frame attaches. Arc asks for the criteria before the layout is drawn.
- **Landmarks.** On a designated property, LPC says awning installation or replacement needs an LPC permit, filed through Portico. Staff criteria for a new fixed storefront awning generally call for a slope, open sides, an open underside, a matte cover in a solid color or vertical stripes, and an unframed skirt no more than 12 inches with the lettering on that skirt. A retractable awning may be straight or curved. These are review criteria, not an approval.

Where installation is sign work, a Licensed Sign Hanger performs or directly supervises it, except where Construction Code §28-415.2 applies. A lit awning also needs the project's licensed electrician. Filing and approval questions stay on [sign permits and shop drawings](/sign-permits-shop-drawings/).

## Materials and process

Commercial awning fabrication is a frame, a cover, a valance, and a way for water to leave the front without sheeting across the sidewalk. The survey checks utilities, clearance, and how the crew will reach the facade.

### Frame

Aluminum or steel tube, welded or bolted, with rafters and arms spaced for the span. A rigid valance is part of the frame. A loose valance hangs below it. Commercial valances are often about 8 to 10 inches. At a landmark the skirt is unframed and no more than 12 inches.

### Covers

Solution-dyed acrylic is the matte woven canvas. Vinyl-coated polyester is smoother and can be welded at the seams. Translucent vinyl is the cover when the face is lit from inside. Metal panels, standing seam or flat, are the unlit alternative. Vertical stripes run from the wall toward the front edge.

### Drainage and access

The code calls for water from a rigid awning, canopy, or marquee to stay off the public walking surface. Flexible covers are treated differently. The drawing notes the pitch, any gap at the wall, and the sidewalk clearance under the frame and under the valance.

### Cut sheets

Landmark work needs material specifications and manufacturer cut sheets. Arc names a fabric fire-retardant only when that product's documentation says so. A color name or a finish sample is not that documentation.

- **01 Survey:** Photos, dimensions, projection, clearance, frame condition, utilities, and access.
- **02 Layout:** Shape, valance, copy, contrast, and sightlines from the sidewalk.
- **03 Drawings:** Awning shop drawings and a material review, with cut sheets when the address is landmarked.
- **04 Coordination:** Landlord, and DOB, DOT, or LPC coordination with the licensed parties. Arc does not file.
- **05 Install:** Fabrication, then installation with a Licensed Sign Hanger and electrician where required.

## Awning questions

### Do storefront awnings need a NYC permit?

The NYC Department of Buildings says to select General Construction for an awning or a replacement canopy. Lettering requires a separate or subsequent Sign work type. Arc coordinates drawings and the required parties and does not file as the licensed applicant. Source: DOB NOW: Build General Construction FAQs. Verify before publishing.

### What is the difference between an awning and a sidewalk canopy?

NYC311 describes a building-attached awning as under Department of Buildings jurisdiction. A pole-supported cover over the sidewalk is a canopy and needs a Department of Transportation permit. Confirm which one the storefront is before drawings go out. Source: NYC311 Canopy Permit. Verify before publishing.

### Does lettering on an awning need its own filing?

The Department of Buildings says lettering requires a separate or subsequent Sign work type. One filing does not automatically cover the frame and the copy. Source: DOB NOW: Build General Construction FAQs. Verify before publishing.

### What if the storefront is landmarked or in a historic district?

The Landmarks Preservation Commission says awning installation or replacement on a designated property needs an LPC permit, filed through Portico. Check the building on the LPC map and the awnings guidance before work starts. Fixed awnings at landmarks are generally expected to slope, with an open underside and lettering on an unframed skirt. Source: LPC Storefronts and the LPC Awnings guidebook. Verify before publishing.

### Can you specify fire-retardant fabric?

Only when the product documentation for that fabric says so. The Landmarks Preservation Commission calls for material specifications and manufacturer cut sheets, and says awnings and canopies must meet the applicable Zoning Resolution, Department of Transportation, and Building Code requirements. Arc does not describe a fabric as fire-retardant without that documentation. Source: LPC Awnings guidebook. Verify before publishing.
