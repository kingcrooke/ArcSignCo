## Name, logo, and lettering

The business name usually sits on the valance, where it reads from the sidewalk. Contrast matters as much as the wording. Lit copy is a sign.

**Try the layout on the facade.** [Preview your awning](/tools/sign-mockup/) in the Free Sign Preview: choose an awning shape, set scale, and place lettering on a storefront photo. It is a concept preview, not a shop drawing or a filing.

Arc does not file permits. Licensed partners file.

Letters, a logo, or both can sit on a rigid valance or on a loose skirt. A light name on a dark valance, or the reverse, is the usual way to keep the words readable. A long line of text sits poorly on the roof slope. At a landmark, the guidance generally puts the lettering on the skirt only.

In commercial and manufacturing districts, non-illuminated copy on an awning the Building Code already allows is the name or the address, in letters no taller than 12 inches and no more than 12 square feet. Residence districts allow less. The address decides which section applies.

Illuminated copy, or copy past the non-illuminated name-or-address allowance, is a sign. In most commercial districts, including C7, that sign may project only 12 inches, or 18 inches if it is double-faced. C6-5 and C6-7 allow up to 8 feet. Filing the sign does not raise the projection limit.

A tall front face, sometimes called a waterfall or a box, is one way to light the graphics from inside. That face is a sign, so it needs electrical coordination. It is not a plain fabric recover. When the front needs a separate lit sign, that sign is [channel letters](/channel-letters/).

- **Department of Buildings.** DOB describes an awning or a replacement canopy under General Construction. DOB treats awning lettering as a separate or later Sign work type. Arc prepares drawings and information for the licensed applicant.
- **Building Code clearances.** For a storefront awning over the sidewalk, the provisions describe a projection of no more than 8 feet beyond the street line, frame clearance of at least 8 feet, a flexible valance that may come down to 7 feet, and an awning box or cover of no more than 12 inches. An awning over a window or door may project no more than 5 feet beyond the street line, and no part may be less than 8 feet above the sidewalk (BC §3202.2.3.2). The awning has to be supported from the building to use those allowances. Confirm the current text for the address.
- **Department of Transportation.** A cover on posts from the entrance to the curb is a sidewalk canopy. NYC311 puts that permit with DOT, not with the building-attached awning path. Width, height, length, and lettering rules are different. Confirm which structure the storefront actually is.
- **Landlord awning criteria.** Leases often limit projection, color, lettering, lighting, and how the frame attaches. Arc asks for the criteria before the layout is drawn.
- **Landmarks.** On a designated property, LPC says awning installation or replacement needs an LPC permit, filed through Portico.
- Staff criteria for a new fixed storefront awning generally call for a slope, open sides, an open underside, a matte cover in a solid color or vertical stripes, and an unframed skirt no more than 12 inches with the lettering on that skirt. A retractable awning may be straight or curved. These are review criteria, not an approval.
- **Landlord and landmark coordination.** Drawings for the licensed applicant. Arc does not file.

Installation is through Arc's licensed partners. §28-415.2 lists cases that may be exempt; whether one applies is confirmed with the licensed applicant during survey. Filing details are on [sign permits and shop drawings](/sign-permits-shop-drawings/).

Fabrication and installation are through Arc's licensed partners. Arc does not file permits or stamp drawings.

## Materials

An awning is a frame, a cover, a valance, and a way for water to leave the front without running across the sidewalk. The survey checks utilities, clearance, and how the crew will reach the front.

### Frame

Aluminum or steel tube, welded or bolted, with rafters and arms spaced to carry the width. A rigid valance is part of the frame. A loose valance hangs below it. Commercial valances are often about 8 to 10 inches. At a landmark, staff criteria generally call for an unframed skirt no more than 12 inches. These are review criteria, not an approval.

### Covers

Solution-dyed acrylic is the matte woven canvas. Vinyl-coated polyester is smoother and can be welded at the seams. Translucent vinyl is the cover when the face is lit from inside. Metal panels, standing seam or flat, are the unlit alternative. Vertical stripes run from the wall toward the front edge.

### Drainage and access

The code calls for water from a rigid awning, canopy, or marquee to stay off the public walking surface. Flexible covers are treated differently. The drawing notes the pitch, any gap at the wall, and the sidewalk clearance under the frame and under the valance.

### Cut sheets

Landmark work needs material specifications and manufacturer cut sheets. Arc names a fabric fire-retardant only when that product's documentation says so. A color name or a finish sample is not that documentation.

## Awning questions

### How are awnings and awning lettering reviewed in NYC?

DOB NOW uses General Construction for an awning and for replacement of an existing canopy, and a Sign filing for lettering that needs a permit. Non-illuminated name or address on a Building Code awning can be up to 12 square feet, with letters up to 12 inches (ZR §32-653 in commercial districts and §42-642 in manufacturing districts). That is a zoning allowance, not the wall-sign permit exemption. Whether the awning and the lettering need DOB filings is confirmed with the licensed applicant. Marquees are filed in BIS. A lit awning also needs the electrical permit, and an illuminated sign that extends past the building line needs the annual illuminated-sign permit. Arc coordinates drawings and the licensed parties the job needs, and does not file as the licensed applicant. Filing details are on the [sign permits and shop drawings](/sign-permits-shop-drawings/) page.

### What is the difference between an awning and a sidewalk canopy?

NYC311 puts a building-attached awning under the Department of Buildings. A pole-supported cover over the sidewalk is a canopy and needs a Department of Transportation permit. Confirm which one the storefront is before drawings go out.

### Does lettering on an awning need its own filing?

Lettering that exceeds the zoning allowance for non-illuminated name or address copy on an awning generally needs its own sign filing, separate from the awning's general construction filing. Lit copy, or copy past the name-or-address allowance, is treated as a sign. Marquees are filed in BIS. A lit awning also needs the electrical permit, and an illuminated sign that extends past the building line needs the annual illuminated-sign permit. Arc coordinates drawings with the licensed applicant and does not file permits or stamp drawings.

### What if the storefront is landmarked or in a historic district?

The Landmarks Preservation Commission says awning installation or replacement on a designated property needs an LPC permit, filed through Portico. Check the building on the LPC map and the awnings guidance before work starts. Fixed awnings at landmarks are generally expected to have a straight slope and open sides, with an open underside and lettering on an unframed skirt.

### Can Arc specify fire-retardant fabric?

Only when the product documentation for that fabric says so. The Landmarks Preservation Commission calls for material specifications and manufacturer cut sheets, and says awnings and canopies must meet the applicable Zoning Resolution, Department of Transportation, and Building Code requirements. Arc does not describe a fabric as fire-retardant without that documentation.

### What should I send for a quote?

Send photos of the storefront, dimensions, frame details if an awning is already there, artwork and the wording, landlord criteria, landmark status, the deadline, and the street address. Email jc@arcsignco.com or arc@arcsignco.com with the project address in the subject line, or call or text (347) 450-2110.

### When is a re-cover enough, and when does the frame need to be new?

A re-cover keeps a sound, straight frame when the projection, the clearance, and the profile can stay. A new frame, or a full replacement, is the path when the frame is bent, rusted, or undersized, or when the drop, the projection, or the shape has to change. Send straight-on and side photos, the projection, the sidewalk clearance, and frame details. Arc coordinates the design, the permit path, and the install with fabrication partners.

### How long does awning fabric last, and how is it cleaned?

Arc does not publish a fabric lifespan or a fabric warranty. Sunbrella's 10-year limited warranty covers listed shade fabrics that become unserviceable from loss of color or strength under normal use, including sunlight, mildew, and atmospheric chemicals. Some Sunbrella products are covered by a shorter warranty. That warranty is the fabric maker's, not Arc's. Sunbrella's care guidance for shade fabric is to hose the cover with clean water about monthly, brush off loose dirt, wash with mild soap or Sunbrella Clean and a soft brush, rinse, and air dry. A thorough cleaning is often every two to three years. See [Sunbrella's shade-fabric cleaning page](https://www.sunbrella.com/clean-sunbrella-shade-fabrics) and [Sunbrella's 10-year limited warranty PDF](https://www.sunbrella.com/media/pdf/sunbrella-10-year-limited-warranty-en-us.pdf).

### Can the awning carry a logo or the business name?

A business name usually sits on the valance or skirt, where it is easiest to read from the sidewalk. A light name on a dark valance, or the reverse, keeps the words readable. At a landmark, staff criteria generally put the lettering on an unframed skirt. Non-illuminated name or address copy on a Building Code awning can be up to 12 square feet, with letters up to 12 inches, in commercial and manufacturing districts. Lit copy, or copy past that allowance, is a sign. Send the artwork and the exact wording with the quote, or note that a logo file will follow.

### What affects how long an awning project takes?

Timing depends on a re-cover versus a new frame, the size, the fabric, lettering, lighting, landlord criteria, and whether Department of Buildings, Department of Transportation, or Landmarks Preservation Commission review applies. Arc does not publish a week count. The 24-hour quote target starts once Arc has photos with measurements (or drawings, if you have them) and the project street address.
