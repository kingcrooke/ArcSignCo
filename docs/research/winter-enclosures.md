# Winter enclosures research

Checked October 21, 2026. This note is for the `/winter-enclosures/` page. It is not a legal opinion. The public page states rules only when a primary source was read. Anything that could not be verified is marked **FAIL** and is not stated as law on the page.

Arc does not file permits; licensed partners file. The page does not publish prices, testimonials, or claims of past Arc enclosure jobs.

## What a New York vestibule looks like

Competitor photos (reference only, not copied) repeat one object: a box frame, a flat or slightly peaked roof, solid fabric in red, navy, black, or a printed wrap, clear vinyl windows, a door on the front or the side, and the business name on the header. Size tiers that show up in those photos are a small entry, a medium lobby, a large cafe or patio room, and a glass entry. Seasonal fabric boxes come down. Glass and aluminum entries are the year-round path.

Those sites show photographs. None of them shows one illustrated chart at one angle, at one scale, split by size, material, door, and whether the setup is seasonal, year-round, private property, or open air. The picker on this page is that chart.

## Top three NYC pages, and what to beat

Reviewed, in order of usefulness for New York:

1. **Signs NYC** — https://signsny.com/outdoor/winter-vestibules/
   - Does well: seasonal versus permanent, materials (aluminum frame, canvas, polycarbonate or acrylic), ADA, removal, and an attempt at Building Code citations. NYC shop.
   - Beat it: the page is a photo gallery plus prose. It publishes prices and testimonials this site will not copy. It says products are code-compliant as a blanket. The section number it uses for the 18-inch limit (§3202.4.2) is not the current section (§3202.3.2.2). It folds the 50-foot and 300-square-foot plastic-veneer limits into §2606.4. Those limits are in §2605.2. It ties NFPA 701 to the storm-enclosure section. Chapter 32 does not name NFPA 701. Its product page treats a permanent unit as Alt-2 or Alt-3. That is not automatic. See FAIL below.

2. **Winter Vestibule (NYC Signs & Awnings)** — https://wintervestibule.com/
   - Does well: the clearest size tiers (small entry, medium lobby, large cafe or patio room, glass entry), seasonal removal and storage, and a readable list of materials (galvanized steel or aluminum, canvas, sheet metal, composite panel, printed vinyl, glass).
   - Beat it: the permit answer is generic and fails for New York City. No citations. Photos only. No sidewalk versus roadway split. No door-type breakdown.

3. **Brooklyn Signs** — https://www.brooklynsigns.com/exterior/vestibules
   - Does well: a “Types of Vestibules” heading, NYC and New Jersey, steel tube, weather fabric, vinyl windows, glass or plexiglass for a unit that stays up, storage, and branding.
   - Beat it: the types section is prose, not a chart. No code. “Any size,” plus a shop minimum that is not a code rule. Permit help is one sentence.

Reviewed and not in the top three:

- **Sign It NY** — https://signitny.com/storefront-vestibule-guide/ — useful field notes (door swing, sidewalk, single versus multi-door, early-season lead time, repair versus replace). No types chart and no code. Fourth.
- **Thatcher Oaks** — https://www.thatcheroaks.com/winter-vestibules-for-restaurants-and-retail-costs-codes-and-installation-timelines — Chicago and northwest Indiana. Costs, energy claims, and public-way permits from that page are **FAIL** if used as New York rules. Not used.

## PASS — rules used on the page

### Temporary storm enclosures (the sidewalk vestibule)

NYC Building Code §3202.3.2, read October 21, 2026 on UpCodes (https://up.codes/s/temporary-storm-enclosures). Codes index: https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/codes/2022-construction-codes.page

- A temporary storm enclosure must comply with the code, including the fire-resistance rating of the building it is appurtenant to, and Chapter 11.
- It may be permitted from November 15 through the following April 15, and it must be removed at the end of that period.
- §3202.3.2.1, eating and drinking establishments: projection of not more than 25 square feet, if a minimum clear unobstructed sidewalk width of 5 feet is maintained, and door maneuvering clearances comply with Chapter 11 and ICC A117.1 Section 404.
- §3202.3.2.2, other establishments: projection of not more than 18 inches (457 mm) beyond the street line.

NYC311, KA-02227 (https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02227): storm enclosures are temporary vestibules, allowed November 15 through April 15. Complaints include no permit, more than 18 inches beyond the street line, and not removed after April 15. The 311 article does not mention the 25-square-foot restaurant allowance. The code text controls. The licensed applicant confirms the measurement.

Administrative Code §28-105.4 (https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCadmin/0-0-0-156291) lists work exempt from a permit. Storm enclosures are not on that list. The page does not claim a seasonal vestibule is permit-free. NYC311 taking “no permit” complaints is consistent with that.

### Dining Out: a new cafe cannot be a sealed room

- New enclosed sidewalk cafes are not allowed. Existing ones had to be built under a DOB permit before March 16, 2020 (Building Code §3111) and lawfully operated under DCWP between March 16, 2016 and March 16, 2020, and must apply for a DOT license and revocable consent. https://www.diningoutnyc.info/enclosed
- A sidewalk cafe may not be fully enclosed. Perimeter maximum 2 feet 6 inches, exclusive of plantings, removable, not affixed. https://www.diningoutnyc.info/rules/sidewalk/materials-basics
- Adopted DOT rules define a roadway cafe as open-air. https://rules.cityofnewyork.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DOT-Notice-of-Adoption-DOT-Dining-Out-NYC-Rules-Accessible.pdf
- NYC311 KA-03604: no fully enclosed structures. Sidewalk setups year-round. Year-round roadway license applications open in early September 2026. https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-03604
- Roadway cafes shall not operate November 30 through March 31 (adopted DOT rules, section on seasonal operation). The operating window stated on the page is April 1 through November 29.
- Both sidewalk and roadway need a DOT license and revocable consent, four-year term. Ground-floor food service with a DOHMH permit. https://www.diningoutnyc.info/apply and the enclosed-cafe page (four-year term is stated there for the enclosed case; the 311 article states the four-year license fee for roadway or sidewalk).

Sidewalk covers (https://www.diningoutnyc.info/rules/sidewalk/materials-extras): removable, wind-resistant, fire-resistant grade. Awning clearance minimum 8 feet, maximum 10 feet. Overhead covering or umbrella minimum 7 feet, maximum 10 feet. Materials include tensile fabric, retractable fabric, fabric panels, and rigid sheet (polycarbonate, panelized sheet metal). A building-attached awning needs DOB. Landmark buildings and historic districts also need LPC for that attachment. Heaters must be approved by DOB and FDNY.

Roadway (https://www.diningoutnyc.info/rules/roadway/materials-extras and the adopted rules):

- Barriers on all sides except the side fronting the restaurant, not affixed, 30 to 42 inches high, at least 4 inches wide, water-filled at 150 pounds per linear foot unless an alternate is approved, interconnected, reflective strip at least 4 inches. Name and logo allowed. No advertising.
- Vertical screening on the roadway-facing side only, not beyond the perimeter, not affixed to the outward face (may mount on top), not within 50 feet of a regulatory or safety sign, maximum 6 feet above the floor, clear visibility, cannot be opaque, removable, wind-resistant, fire-resistant grade.
- Overhead coverings 7 to 10 feet, not beyond the outer edge, fire-resistant grade, weighted base. Polycarbonate is listed.
- Graphics: name, logo, menu, or sponsor, maximum 10 percent of the surface, not self-illuminated, flashing, or moving. Menu and hours are not permitted on the barrier or vertical screening.

A sealed room inside the property line is a DOB filing, not a Dining Out license. Buildings Bulletin 2024-005 says a DOB Temporary Use Permit is not required for the dining setup itself when the Dining Out license and revocable consent are in place, and that some other work still needs DOB permits. Electrical work is filed by a licensed master electrician. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/buildings/bldgs_bulletins/bb_2024-005.pdf

### Heat

Buildings Bulletin 2024-005, section on natural-gas-fired radiant heaters, read from the bulletin PDF:

- Open-flame natural gas heaters and propane heaters are not permitted in sidewalk, roadway, and outdoor cafes.
- Natural-gas-fired radiant heaters may be allowed for sidewalk cafes or outdoor cafes, and are not permitted for roadway cafes.
- Units must be approved by DOB and FDNY.
- Gas work needs an approved LAA or alteration filing and a DOB permit from a licensed master plumber. FDNY permits and Certificates of Fitness are required before operation.
- Electric heat: licensed master electrician, electrical filing and permit. The bulletin says electrical work cannot be performed without an approved electrical filing and permit.

The page tells people not to plan on propane. The 2020 Open Restaurants heater sheets and the Counsel to the Mayor Heaters FAQ describe the temporary program, including propane. Using those as the current Dining Out rule is **FAIL**.

### Landmarks

63 RCNY §2-23, amendment adopted May 7, 2024, effective June 24, 2024. Text: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCrules/0-0-0-113325. Notice: https://rules.cityofnewyork.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Final-Roadbed-Cafe-Rule-05132024-1.pdf

- A sidewalk or roadway cafe that complies with 34 RCNY Chapter 5 does not need an LPC application, certificate, permit, report, or approval.
- Enclosed sidewalk cafes are not covered by that sidewalk-cafe paragraph.
- If a component (awning, overhead covering, heat lamp, light, or conduit) is physically attached to the building, penetrates the facade or storefront, affects an interior landmark, or attaches to or penetrates historic paving (bluestone, granite, or cast-iron vault light), that component needs an LPC approval before it is installed.
- LPC storefronts page, read October 21, 2026: https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/applications/storefronts.page. “Any change to the exterior of a storefront requires a permit.” The same page still says an unenclosed sidewalk cafe needs a permit from LPC and from DOT, and that applications go through Portico. That sidewalk-cafe sentence is older than §2-23. The page states both, and tells the reader to confirm the address. A vestibule attached to a landmark facade is a storefront change.

LPC §2-05 (temporary signs, banners, artwork, kiosks) is **FAIL** as the vestibule rule. It is not used.

### Plastics and fabric

§2606.4 specifications for light-transmitting plastics (UpCodes chapter 26, https://up.codes/viewer/new_york_city/nyc-building-code-2022/chapter/26/plastic):

- Self-ignition temperature 650°F or greater, ASTM D1929.
- Smoke-developed index not greater than 450, ASTM E84 or UL 723, or maximum average smoke density rating not greater than 75, ASTM D2843.
- Class CC1 or CC2, ASTM D635.

§2605.2 exterior plastic veneer (not §2606.4): must comply with §2606.4, must not be attached above 50 feet above grade, sections must not exceed 300 square feet and must be separated by at least 4 feet vertically, plus fireblocking. Type VB exception. Citing 50 feet and 300 square feet as §2606.4 is **FAIL**.

§2607.1: light-transmitting plastic exterior wall panels are not permitted in Groups A-1, A-2, H, I-2, and I-3. Restaurants are often A-2. Whether a seasonal sidewalk storm enclosure is an “exterior wall panel” of the building was not confirmed in a DOB bulletin read for this page. The page does not say plastics are banned on a restaurant vestibule, and it does not say they are automatically allowed. The licensed applicant confirms it.

§3105.3 awnings and canopies (https://up.codes/viewer/new_york_city/nyc-building-code-2022/chapter/31/special-construction): frames of noncombustible material. Fabric must meet NFPA 701 Test Method 1 or 2, or have a flame spread index not greater than 25 under ASTM E84 or UL 723, or the cover is plastic per §2605, sheet metal, or an equivalent. Exception above that section: canopies projecting over public rights-of-way governed by Title 19 and DOT rules. §3202.3.2 does not name NFPA 701. Dining Out requires “fire-resistant grade materials” and does not name NFPA 701 on the pages read. Claiming every vestibule canvas must be NFPA 701 because of Chapter 32 is **FAIL**. If the enclosure includes an awning or canopy, §3105.3 applies to that awning or canopy.

§2606.10 says awnings of light-transmitting plastics follow §3105 and Chapter 32, and patio covers of light-transmitting plastics comply with §2606.

### Permanent work and job type

DOB job types: https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/industry/obtaining-a-permit.page and https://www.nyc.gov/assets/buildings/pdf/job-types-in-dob-now.pdf

- DOB NOW job-type sheet (industry session, updated February 16, 2021): Alteration-CO (formerly Alt-1) is the job type if any of these is yes: the alteration has to meet New Building requirements, it is inconsistent with the current certificate of occupancy, it changes occupancy or use, it is a major change to exits, or it changes the number of stories. Alt-2 and Alt-3 both map to Alteration.
- A permanent vestibule is not automatically a minor alteration. The licensed applicant confirms the job type. A blanket “every permanent vestibule is Alt-2 or Alt-3” is **FAIL**.

### Lettering

The name on the header is the usual layout in the reference photos. This page does not copy the awning lettering allowance in ZR §32-653 (12 square feet, 12-inch letters) onto a vestibule. If the lettering is a sign, the licensed applicant confirms the filing. Dining Out graphic limits above apply to Dining Out setups only.

## FAIL — do not state these as New York rules

- Signs NYC §3202.4.2 as the current 18-inch section. Current text is §3202.3.2.2.
- Signs NYC treating the 50-foot height and 300-square-foot area as §2606.4. Those limits are §2605.2. §2606.4 is the plastic specification (650°F, smoke, CC1/CC2).
- Signs NYC pinning NFPA 701 to Chapter 32 storm enclosures.
- Signs NYC product-page claim that a permanent vestibule is Alt-2 or Alt-3.
- Any claim that a code-sized seasonal vestibule needs no permit. §28-105.4 does not list it, and NYC311 takes “no permit” complaints.
- wintervestibule.com saying most cities have no specific code or that a vestibule usually needs no permit, applied to New York City.
- Brooklyn Signs “minimum 5 feet wide by 7 feet long” as a code minimum. That is a shop note.
- Thatcher Oaks costs, energy savings, and permit notes, presented as New York.
- 2020 Open Restaurants, Emergency Executive Order 153, the FDNY LPG delivery sheet, and the Counsel to the Mayor Heaters FAQ, used as current Dining Out heater law.
- LPC §2-05 as the rule for a vestibule.
- An invented square-foot limit for the name on a vestibule header, copied from the awning zoning section.
- Competitor prices and testimonials. Not published. Signs NYC price bands seen on the product page are left in this note only so a later edit does not “fill them in.”
